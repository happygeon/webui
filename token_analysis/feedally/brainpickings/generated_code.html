Vera Rubin: Pioneering Astronomer and Her Legacy

Early Life and Education

Rubin was born on February 28, 1928, in Washington D.C., and showed early interest in science. She earned her Bachelor of Arts degree from Vassar College in 1949 and later a Master’s degree in Astronomy from Georgetown University in 1954.

Career Highlights

Despite facing discrimination as a woman in a male-dominated field, Rubin persevered. She joined the Carnegie Institution for Science in 1959 and spent most of her career there. Her observations led to the discovery of galactic rotation curves, which suggested that galaxies contain more mass than can be accounted for by visible matter, thus providing strong evidence for dark matter.

Legacy

Rubin's work has had a profound impact on our understanding of the universe. Her findings continue to guide astronomical research today, and she remains a role model for women in science. In recognition of her contributions, she received numerous awards, including the National Medal of Science in 1993.
